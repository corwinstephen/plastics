There’s a distinct archetype in housing now that I’ve been calling the “Boomer Special”

It’s a house that has been both upgraded and neglected simultaneously. Or rather: the upgrades contribute to the neglect.

In a boomer special, the marked up original floors have been replaced with LVP, but not throughout the home, and never more than one room at a time, and each phase used a slightly different style of flooring, and the transitions between them are either marked by a plastic transition strip or left as an unfinished gap to be completed “when I get around to it.”

In a boomer special, every appliance has been modified with a plastic dollar store contraption. The toilets all have broken pneumatic buttons that were in theory supposed to save water, but being dollar store quality, they broke within a week and now everyone has to use the high flush option every time. Net result: no water savings, and a hole in the side of the toilet where the proper handle used to be.

In a boomer special, things that can be maintained are deemed “old” and are instead thrown out and replaced with plastic simulacra that cannot be maintained, and whose lifespan is a fraction of the original’s. All appliances have been replaced with touch button devices from Alibaba that are broken even when they’re working

In a boomer special, nothing is usable for its original purpose. The garage was converted to a bedroom. The fireplaces have fake logs and can’t burn wood. The backyard is has been stripped of all natural elements because:

In a boomer special, low maintenance is the only priority, so anything that requires effort has been removed, regardless of how rewarding it is. No plants, no gardens, no natural materials that require sanding or polishing.

And finally, in a boomer special, all these things are seen as “improvements”, and they expect you to pay a premium for them.
