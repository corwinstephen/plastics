There is religion, which is spiritual, and there is religious thinking, which is a style of thinking meant for talking about spirituality, but which has bled through to secular conversation and is wreaking havoc on our ability to think clearly.

Definition

If you dedicate yourself to a cause but cannot give a definite answer to the question, “How much is enough?”, you are guilty of religious thinking.

Examples

In spiritual religion, this typically manifests as follows:

Q: How much good do I need to do in order to not be a sinner? A: You should do as much good as possible, but no matter how much good you do, you will always be a sinner.

Q: How much do I need to meditate to become enlightened? A: You should seek enlightenment in every aspect of your life, but you can never fully achieve enlightenment.

In secular discussion, it looks more like this:

Q: How much do we need to cut taxes? A: You should always cut taxes, and no matter how much you cut them, it will never be enough.

Q: How many government services should we provide? A: All government services are inherently inadequate and should be better funded and more comprehensive.

Q: How much “healing/learning/atoning” do we need to do before we can move past [historical tragedy]? A: You should factor atonement for [historical tragedy] into everything you do, but no matter how much you do, you can never actually be healed, and the debt can never be repaid.

Religious thinking detaches you from the concrete facts that are required to make fruitful decisions. If you cannot measure your progress toward an achievable goal, all you’re doing is wasting time and money.
