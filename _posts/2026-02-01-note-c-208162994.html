Stuck culture gets a lot of talk these days. Here’s my theory on why it’s happening:

Culture evolves at the margins. Nobody pulls something genuinely new out of a vacuum. Ideas morph slowly over time, sometimes intentionally, but more often by accident. When people set out to do one thing—make music of a certain genre, design a house, shoot a film—tiny variations in the tools used and the people involved and the habits of consumers and audiences used to have unpredictable outcomes that were occasionally loved and celebrated. Once upon a time, subpar Russian manufacturing technique lead to the creation of a camera whose images were distorted with a blue-green hue. The images were initially labeled poor quality, but eventually they picked up a following of people who actually liked the look. Eventually it became so popular that some app developers created an app to mimic the look on digital photos via “filters”. That app is Instagram.

That Russian camera existed in the margins. It didn’t excel at anything that anyone would have thought to optimize for. Instead, it created new culture. It made a new category for itself altogether. A category which today is specifically sought out and optimized for.

The is no limit to these undiscovered categories. At least not in theory. But increasingly, we live in a world that makes them impossible to discover. This is because the quantified, digitized, mechanized world has no margins.

When we build houses with Home Depot parts and plant lawns with Home Depot grass and grow food with Monsanto seed and buy food from grocery stores where every item needs to be labeled and registered and have a scannable SKU and make music with digital instruments with a finite number of preprogrammed sounds and buy products that all come from the same factories, there is no possibility for the kinds of human scale variation that creates new culture. A man who sets out to build a guitar may make a random choice, or a mistake, or introduce a feature which eventually leads to the creation of an entirely new use for the instrument. But the guy who buys a Stratocaster from Guitar Center has an all but predetermined destiny.

Systemification, the thing has been calling the machine, blocks the evolution of culture. That’s why we’re stuck.
